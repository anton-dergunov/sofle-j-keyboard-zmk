Sofle and Cornix layouts

Each proposal below shows every layer twice: the Sofle on the left, the Cornix on the right, drawn as they sit on the desk. Rings mark keys that change from today; tap or hover a ringed key to see what it did before and why it moves. Nothing here is flashed yet.

Your requirements, as I understood them
  1. The two boards should feel the same. The Cornix is "a Sofle without the number row". The Cornix can change freely; the Sofle only a little.
  2. Stay close to a Mac laptop keyboard. QWERTY, the usual punctuation spots, no Dvorak/Colemak, no home-row mods or other tricks.
  3. macOS and iPadOS first. ⌃ ⌥ ⌘ in Mac order on both halves; Linux is rare, Windows avoided.
  4. Thumbs: Space on both, Enter off. Alternating Space cured the thumb pain; Enter moved to the left pinky. Enter on only one side still bothers you.
  5. Symmetry. Modifiers and Shift on both halves. One half alone must reach every layer, so a dead half never locks you out and you can hold on one side and type on the other.
  6. System layer is hard to reach on purpose: hold M1, then M2.
  7. The Nav layer works well. Arrows on a held layer are fine; a little asymmetry is fine.
  1. F1–F10 are almost never used. They can move to another layer or go.
  2. Esc is rare. A key combination is fine, as long as it fits the number-row logic.
  3. Daily use: coding, data science, office work, a tablet on the sofa. No gaming.
  4. Knobs: both Cornix knobs turn volume. Their push-buttons stay as they are; using them for keys is a last resort.
  5. Desk switch (Personal / Work): plan where it would go on the Cornix, wire it later.
  6. Pictures in the style of the Sofle note: Mac symbols, light and dark, only the keys that exist.

The hard questions you named: where the digits go on the Cornix; how the Nav layer is organised; how layers are switched; where Esc, Tab, Enter, Shift, ⌫ and \ sit on the base layer; the bottom row; and the System layer.
