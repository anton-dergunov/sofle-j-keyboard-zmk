Sofle carrying case concepts

Five rough designs for carrying both Sofle-J Choc halves without anything pressing the keys. Each is modelled around a stand-in keyboard built from PandaKB's case model and your measurements, so the sizes, weights and clearances below come from the models, not from guesses. Pick 2–3 to develop into print-ready parts.

In the renders the light grey parts are the keyboard, teal is printed nylon (MJF PA12), orange is flexible TPU and dark blue is a silicone band.

Concept 1

Spacer ring + straps

PA12 · 39 g150 × 125 × 38 mm1 printed part + 2 straps

A single nylon ring sits between the two rims and keeps the keycaps 2 mm apart. Short skirts drop 4 mm over each case wall so the halves can't slide. Two silicone straps hold the stack together, and the keyboard's own case bottoms are the outside of the package.

Good

  • Light, with the sides fully closed
  • One simple part, cheap to print
  • The load runs rim to rim, never through the keys

Watch

  • The case bottoms take any scuffs
  • Two straps to put on and take off
Concept 2

TPU bumper

TPU · 66 g153 × 126 × 40 mm1 printed part

The same idea in flexible TPU, stretched over both halves like a phone bumper. It covers the full height of both case walls, wraps 2 mm under the bottoms at four corners, and has thicker corner guards where backpack knocks land. Side windows save weight.

Good

  • Holds by itself, nothing else to carry
  • Absorbs knocks at corners and edges
  • Quick on and off

Watch

  • The fit depends on the TPU print, so a test section first is wise
  • The heaviest of the open-bottom options
Concept 3

Corner clips + band

PA12 · 9 g150 × 122 × 38 mm5 clips + 1 band

Five 22 mm sections of the ring sit at the corners, the thumb tip and the front edge. They keep the gap that protects the keys, and one band holds the stack together. This is the lightest option and suits carrying the stack inside your felt sleeve.

Good

  • Under 10 g, almost no added size
  • Very cheap to print

Watch

  • Open sides: a pen or cable can reach the keys
  • Five small parts to keep track of
Concept 4

Snap-on lids

PA12 · 2 × 33 geach half 150 × 125 × 20 mm2 printed parts

A thin lid for each half. It rests on the rim, 1 mm above the keycaps, and its skirt grips the case wall. A finger notch at the front lifts it off. The capped halves go side by side in the felt sleeve, as now, or back to back for a smaller footprint.

Good

  • Keeps the thin, side-by-side layout of your sleeve
  • Each half is protected on its own and can travel alone

Watch

  • Two separate parts
  • Stacked back to back it is as thick as the face-to-face designs
Concept 5

Hinged clamshell

PA12 · 79 g159 × 125 × 41 mm2 trays + 4 magnets

Two shallow trays, each holding one half, joined by a hinge along the inner edges. It closes like a book, and two pairs of 8 × 2 mm magnets on the pinky side keep it shut. Opened flat, the halves sit side by side in their trays, so the case can double as the base you type on.

Good

  • Covers everything, including the case bottoms
  • One object; opens straight into typing position

Watch

  • The largest and heaviest, though still well under 200 g
  • The hinge and magnets need careful detail design

What the models assume

  • The newer PandaKB case (August 2025): 146.3 × 121.2 mm, rim at 10.6 mm, display column at 14.0 mm. The right case is an exact mirror of the left, so the halves line up face to face.
  • Keycap tops at 18 mm, from your measurement. Face to face, the rims sit 16.8 mm apart and the keycap tops 2 mm apart. Every design bears on the rims, never on the keys.
  • Fit 0.35 mm around the case wall and 1.6 mm nylon walls, typical for MJF/SLS print services. Weights use 1.01 g/cm³ for PA12 and 1.20 g/cm³ for TPU.
  • These are concept shapes. Snap fits, the hinge, magnet pockets and strap grooves get designed properly for the 2–3 you shortlist, together with print-service quotes.
